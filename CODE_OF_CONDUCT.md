# Code of Conduct

LeaseOS is used to dispatch drivers, record hours of service and carry compliance
evidence. Mistakes here reach people on the road, so the standard is a working
professional one: direct about the work, careful with the people.

## Disagreement is expected

Technical disagreement is normal and welcome. Say plainly when you think a design,
a decision or a piece of code is wrong, and say why.

Critique the decision, the code or the argument — not the person who made it. "This
query is not tenant-scoped" is useful. "You never think about security" is not, and it
does not make the query any safer.

Being right does not license contempt. Someone who asks an obvious question, makes a
beginner's mistake, or misreads the codebase gets an answer, not an audience.

## Not acceptable

- Harassment, in the repository or anywhere it spills over from it.
- Discrimination or demeaning remarks about who someone is.
- Threats or intimidation, including toward people outside the project.
- Deliberately publishing someone's private information.
- Persistently pushing past a clear "no" on a decision that has been made and explained.

## Confidentiality

Much of what passes through this project is not ours to publish.

Do not post, in an issue, pull request, commit or discussion:

- customer or operational data;
- employee or driver personal information — licences, medical records, addresses, pay;
- credentials or secrets of any kind;
- unpublished details of a security vulnerability (see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)).

When you need to show a real case, sanitize it first. A redacted example that makes the
point is worth more than a real one that cannot be shared.

## Claims need evidence

For anything touching security, safety or regulatory compliance, bring evidence: the
reproduction, the failing test, the regulation and section, the log line.

"I think this is unsafe" opens a conversation. "This is unsafe" closes one, and it should
only be said when you can show it. Assertions in these areas get acted on, and acting on a
wrong one costs real work — or, worse, moves a real risk out of view.

## Enforcement

Repository administrators may remove content, and may restrict or remove access, in
response to conduct described here. To raise a concern, contact an administrator
privately; reports are handled discreetly.
