2. Alberta intra-provincial 160 km exemption
For Alberta's provincial daily-log exemption, all applicable conditions must remain true: the driver does not operate beyond a 160 km
radius from the home terminal; returns to the home terminal and is released from work within 15 hours from the start of the work shift;
and the carrier retains accurate start/release time records for 6 months.
When any condition stops applying, LeaseOS must change the day to LOG_REQUIRED. Alberta's carrier-training material says the
driver must commence a daily log and record the total on-duty hours accumulated during the immediately preceding 7 days.
3. Federal Canadian 160 km exemption
Federal section 81 exempts the driver from the normal RODS requirement only when the driver drives, or is instructed to drive, within a
160 km radius of the home terminal; returns to the home terminal each day to begin at least 8 consecutive hours off duty; the
carrier keeps accurate/legible cycle and on-duty records plus supporting documents for at least 6 months; and the driver is not
operating under a permit (unless the regulation/exemption provides otherwise).
Important distinction: The federal rule is tied to the home terminal and its statutory conditions. LeaseOS should not replace this with a
generic '160 km from current location' test.
4. When the driver goes beyond 160 km
Crossing the applicable radius, or otherwise losing an exemption condition, does not create extra driving time. It changes the
record-keeping state. LeaseOS should immediately require a full RODS/daily log for that day and reconstruct the earlier duty
status/history needed by the applicable rule.

6. Sleeper berth
For a single driver under federal Canadian rules, qualifying sleeper time may be accumulated in no more than two periods when neither
is shorter than 2 hours and the two total at least 10 hours. The paired periods must also satisfy the driving, on-duty and elapsed-time
tests in section 18. Sleeper berth changes the calculation; it is not a blanket extension of legal hours.
LeaseOS: store both qualifying sleeper periods, pair ID, start/end timestamps, total duration, preceding/following driving,
preceding/following on-duty time, elapsed-time calculation, and VALID/PENDING/INVALID result.

7. Federal ELD requirements and exceptions
Transport Canada states that federally regulated commercial drivers who must complete RODS must use a compliant,
third-party-certified ELD. Regulatory exceptions include specified permit/exemption operations, qualifying rentals of 30 days or less,
pre-2000 model-year vehicles, and drivers who are not required to keep RODS.
The vehicle should carry the required ELD information packet, including user instructions, data-transfer instructions, malfunction
instructions and enough blank RODS for at least 15 days. Carrier RODS and supporting documents are retained for at least 6 months.
LeaseOS product boundary: LeaseOS may manage HOS workflows and integrate with an ELD, but it must not present itself as the
legally required Canadian ELD unless the actual hardware/software implementation is tested and certified under the Transport Canada
framework.

10. Canada interprovincial / provincial-border operation
A carrier that is an extra-provincial undertaking is governed by the federal Canadian HOS regime for covered operations. LeaseOS
should therefore determine the carrier's regulatory status, not simply switch to each province's intra-provincial HOS rule whenever GPS
crosses a provincial border.
Routing behavior: Before dispatch, classify the trip as INTRAPROVINCIAL, INTERPROVINCIAL/TERRITORIAL or INTERNATIONAL.
Preserve the classification in the job/trip audit record. A border-crossing route should trigger federal HOS/ELD validation before the
driver accepts dispatch.
11. Canada-U.S. international operation
Canadian drivers entering the United States must comply with applicable U.S. FMCSA HOS/ELD requirements while operating in the
U.S. FMCSA guidance states that a Canada/Mexico-domiciled driver operating in the U.S. who must use RODS for more than 8 days in
any 30-day period is subject to the U.S. ELD rule unless another exception applies.
FMCSA also recognizes the U.S. 150 air-mile short-haul exception for qualifying cross-border operations when all U.S. short-haul
conditions are met. Current FMCSA cross-border guidance describes return to the normal work reporting location and release within 14
hours as part of that exception.
Do not reuse Canada's 160 km test for the U.S. The U.S. short-haul radius is 150 air miles and has its own statutory conditions.
LeaseOS should treat the border as a jurisdiction transition and evaluate U.S. rules independently.
At/Before Canada-U.S. border
LeaseOS check
Driver's current Canadian RODS/history
Preserve/export the history needed to establish prior duty status and make it available for inspection.
