2. Alberta provincial 160 km daily-log exemption
A daily log is not required only when all applicable Alberta conditions are satisfied:
 driver stays within a 160 km radius of the home terminal;
 driver returns to the home terminal and is released from work within 15 hours from the start of the work shift; and
 carrier keeps accurate start/end time records for at least 6 months.
If a condition fails, the driver must begin a daily log and account for the required preceding duty history. The 160 km provision is a
record-keeping exemption only.
3. Federal 160 km record-of-duty-status / ELD exemption
Under federal rules, the normal record-of-duty-status requirement does not apply when the driver is instructed to drive within a 160
km radius of the home terminal, returns there each day to begin at least 8 consecutive hours off duty, and the carrier keeps
accurate cycle/on-duty records and supporting documents for at least 6 months. Permit/exemption status can alter eligibility.
A qualifying federal 160 km driver can therefore be exempt from the normal ELD/daily record, but the carrier still needs records
proving compliance.

5. Sleeper berth - LeaseOS logic
Sleeper berth does not authorize exceeding legal driving/on-duty limits. For a single driver under federal rules, qualifying off-duty
time may be split into no more than two sleeper periods; neither can be shorter than 2 hours and together they must total at least 10
hours. The regulation still applies combined 13-hour driving and 14-hour on-duty controls and a special calculation for the 16-hour
elapsed-time rule.
Do not make sleeper use an automatic ELD trigger. Determine log/ELD status separately. When split sleeper is used, preserve
precise timestamps and validate both periods before treating the split as compliant.

9. Canada-wide implementation rule
Canada does not use one identical provincial short-haul/logbook rule for every intra-provincial carrier. Provinces and territories
regulate intra-provincial undertakings; federal rules govern extra-provincial undertakings. LeaseOS should select the jurisdiction
profile from the carrier's legal operating status and trip, not merely the driver's current GPS province.
Production profiles should separately version Alberta, British Columbia, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, Quebec, New
Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Prince Edward Island, Newfoundland and Labrador, Yukon, Northwest Territories and Nunavut. Each
should store applicability thresholds, daily/work-shift limits, cycles, radius/log exemptions, ELD rules, special/oilfield permits,
north-of-60 rules, retention periods and required fields.
